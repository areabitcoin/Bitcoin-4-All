##  Lesson Content

### The Dollar Has Lost 97% of Its Value

Since the creation of the Federal Reserve in 1913, the US dollar has lost approximately **97% of its purchasing power**. What cost $1 in 1913 would cost over $30 today. This is not an accidentit's a feature of fiat money.

### What is Fiat Money?

**Fiat** comes from Latin meaning "let it be done" or "by decree." Fiat money has value because governments say it does, not because it's backed by anything tangible like gold. Since 1971, when Nixon ended the gold standard, all major currencies are fiat.

### The Hidden Tax: Inflation

Inflation is often called the "hidden tax" because it steals your purchasing power without you realizing it:

- **Savers are punished** - Your savings lose value over time
- **Debtors benefit** - Debt becomes easier to pay off
- **Governments benefit most** - They can print money to pay debts

### The Cantillon Effect

Named after 18th-century economist Richard Cantillon, this effect explains how:

1. **New money enters the economy unevenly**
2. **Those closest to the money printer benefit first** (banks, large corporations, governments)
3. **By the time money reaches ordinary people**, prices have already risen
4. **Result**: Wealth transfers from the poor to the rich

##  Key Takeaways

1. **The US dollar has lost 97% of its value since 1913**
2. **Fiat money has value only by government decree**
3. **Inflation is a hidden tax on your savings**
4. **The Cantillon Effect makes the rich richer**
5. **The current monetary system benefits those closest to the money printer**
